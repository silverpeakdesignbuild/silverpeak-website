Modern Craftsman Whole House Build

Modern Craftsman Whole House Build in Greenwood

This Greenwood property was completed in two major phases. Silver Peak built an 850-square-foot rear addition in 2008, then took the structurally compromised 100-year-old primary house down to its foundation in 2018 and rebuilt it as a new, approximately 3,000-square-foot modern Craftsman home with Scandinavian influence.

The completed home includes solar panels, an EV charger, an open kitchen, dining and living area, a basement ADU, a primary suite, multiple bathrooms, custom storage, a distinctive shed, and landscaped outdoor spaces.

Exterior and energy upgrades

Modern craftsman exterior with efficient upgrades

The home was reimagined with a modern craftsman look that kept a familiar Seattle neighborhood character while giving the property a cleaner and more updated presence. Strong exterior color, crisp trim, and a balanced front elevation help create the curb appeal.

Solar panels, an EV charger, custom landscaping, and a distinctive front-yard shed with a living roof make the exterior feel intentionally designed rather than simply refreshed.

Kitchen craftsmanship

Custom kitchen details built for everyday use

The kitchen includes a concealed breakfast bar cabinet, quartz work surfaces, under-cabinet outlets, LED lighting, custom Barcelona tile, and quartz window sills. These details keep the room clean visually while making the kitchen more useful day to day.

The backsplash was treated as a design feature, with outlets hidden below the cabinets so the tile can read as one continuous finish.

Main level planning

Open kitchen, dining, and living area

The main level was designed around one connected kitchen, dining, and living space. The kitchen acts as the center of the home, while the dining and living areas remain visually connected to patio access, natural light, and the yard.

This layout makes the home feel larger, brighter, and better suited for entertaining, family life, and everyday movement through the main floor.

Interior warmth

Walnut floors and a custom family room bar

Real walnut floors add warmth and character through the interior. At the back of the home, the family room includes a custom bar designed as a built-in feature rather than a standard cabinet installation.

The bar uses butcher block, open shelving, and metal supports that coordinate with the faucet finish, giving the room a crafted and personal detail.

Lower-level living

Basement ADU with flexible living space

The lower level includes a basement ADU with its own living area, kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, and laundry function. It adds a separate living environment within the property rather than feeling like leftover basement space.

One distinctive feature is the stained original concrete floor with a clear finish, preserving part of the home’s older structure while giving the ADU a unique finish.

Private suite

Primary suite, bathroom, and built-in storage

The primary suite was designed to feel grand and complete, with high ceilings, a walnut beam, layered lighting, and a connected bathroom and walk-in closet. The bathroom includes a long shower layout, bench, double vanity, and two shower heads.

Deep recessed medicine cabinets, interior lighting, outlets, shoe storage, and tailored closet zones show how the private spaces were planned with the same care as the public rooms.

Bathroom work

Bathrooms and private room finishes

Bathroom and bedroom spaces were treated as part of the full-home scope. Custom tile, updated fixtures, storage planning, and clean finish work help the private rooms feel consistent with the rest of the renovation.

The bathrooms show the level of detail that can go into smaller rooms, where tile, glass, fixtures, cabinetry, and transitions all need to be carefully coordinated.

Outdoor living

Outdoor living, stonework, and landscaping

The backyard was treated as another major part of the renovation. The outdoor space includes a stamped concrete patio designed to resemble cobblestone, basalt stone retaining walls, and an integrated stone bench built into the wall.

A bamboo garden with integrated lighting adds privacy, texture, and atmosphere, helping the yard feel like an extension of the renovated home.

Exterior Restoration and Bathroom/Laundry Renovation

Tudor Exterior Restoration and Bathroom/Laundry Renovation in Licton Springs

This Licton Springs project restored a distinctive Tudor home while also improving its bathroom and laundry spaces. Because this old-world architecture is unusual in Seattle, the curved rooflines, brickwork, stucco, and trim were especially important to preserve.

The exterior work included cleaning and re-tuckpointing the brick, replacing deteriorated mortar, removing rotten wood, rebuilding straight and curved trim, repairing stucco in the gables and rounded entry, and renewing the laundry-room entrance with a new door.

Alongside the exterior restoration, the project scope also included bathroom and laundry improvements to support better everyday use. Together, the work paired practical interior updates with a careful exterior restoration that made the whole property feel more complete.

Project Details

Explore how masonry repair, stucco, trim, entries, and interior utility spaces were coordinated to restore the home as one complete property.

Tudor house character

A rare Tudor home with strong architectural identity

This Tudor home reflects a Northern European architectural style that feels uncommon in Seattle. The steep gables, brick walls, and distinctive trim made the home stand out right away, which is why the remodel needed to preserve the character instead of flattening it into a generic exterior update.

That uncommon character is part of what made the project memorable. The goal was not just to make the home look newer, but to restore the visual identity that already made it special.

Brick restoration

Brick cleaning and re-tuck-pointing throughout the exterior

A major part of the project was restoring the brick envelope. The mortar was badly aged and, in some areas, the bricks were close to failing, so the masonry had to be cleaned and the joints carefully rebuilt.

Re-tuck-pointing the brick gave the walls structural stability again while also sharpening the look of the exterior. It was a lot of work, but it made the house read as solid, clean, and well cared for.

Stucco and trim restoration

Gables, stucco, and detailed trim rebuilt with care

The gables and upper wall sections also needed significant attention. Rotten wood was removed, trim pieces were rebuilt, and the stucco was redone so the Tudor detailing would feel crisp again instead of tired or patched together.

The rounded and angled wood details required especially careful custom cuts. Those custom cuts matter because they help the finished home feel intentional and true to the original design language.

Laundry access update

Entry improvements and a rebuilt laundry-room door

The remodel also included work at the laundry-room entry. This door area was rebuilt and fitted with a new door, improving both function and the finished appearance of the service-side access point.

This part of the project ties the practical bathroom and laundry scope back into the exterior work. Even the utility-focused areas were treated as part of the overall finished home, not as an afterthought.

Side elevation and chimney

Side-wall restoration carried the same level of finish

On the side of the house, the brickwork and mortar joints were restored around the chimney and adjacent wall surfaces. This elevation shows how the restoration continued consistently around the house, not just on the front facade.

The result is a home that feels cohesive from multiple angles. The restoration is visible in the masonry, the trim lines, and the way the older structure now reads as strong and complete again.

Rear elevation

Rear and back-gable views complete the full-house exterior refresh

The back of the house and rear gable also benefited from the same restoration approach. By the time the project was finished, the difference from the original condition was dramatic.

The completed restoration makes the older home feel structurally renewed, visually cohesive, and carefully preserved. That transformation is the clearest takeaway from this project.

Bathroom Remodel

Accessible Shower Bathroom Remodel in Seattle

This Seattle bathroom remodel was planned around long-term comfort, ease of use, and a calm shower experience. A walk-in shower, built-in bench, integrated support rails, neutral tile, and clean detailing create a space that feels safe, durable, and refined.

Comfort-focused shower layout

The shower layout was the main design priority. The built-in bench adds comfort and flexibility, while the walk-in configuration improves access and ease of movement. The shower feels spacious and useful without relying on unnecessary decoration.

Integrated support features

Support rails were included as part of the overall design. Instead of looking like add-ons, they are placed within the shower layout to support daily use while keeping the bathroom cohesive. This makes the space more comfortable for long-term living.

Neutral tile and maintenance

The neutral tile palette keeps the bathroom calm and bright. It also supports easier maintenance and a timeless finish. Clean tile installation, simple lines, and practical materials help the room perform well over time.

Residential warmth

A major strength of the project is that usability was handled without losing warmth. The bathroom supports comfort and safety, but it still feels like a residential space with a thoughtful design direction.

The completed remodel is durable, calm, and carefully planned. It demonstrates how a bathroom can be designed for everyday convenience, future flexibility, and long-term comfort while still feeling polished and elegant.

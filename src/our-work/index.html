Modern Craftsman Whole House Build

Modern Craftsman Whole House Build in Greenwood

This Greenwood property was completed in two major phases. Silver Peak built an 850-square-foot rear addition in 2008, then took the structurally compromised 100-year-old primary house down to its foundation in 2018 and rebuilt it as a new, approximately 3,000-square-foot modern Craftsman home with Scandinavian influence.

The completed home includes solar panels, an EV charger, an open kitchen, dining and living area, a basement ADU, a primary suite, multiple bathrooms, custom storage, a distinctive shed, and landscaped outdoor spaces.

CategoryWhole House Build
LocationGreenwood, Seattle, Washington

Project Details

Explore the major exterior, interior, ADU, custom-detail, and outdoor-living decisions that shaped the finished property.

Exterior and energy upgrades

Modern craftsman exterior with efficient upgrades

The home was reimagined with a modern craftsman look that kept a familiar Seattle neighborhood character while giving the property a cleaner and more updated presence. Bold exterior color, crisp trim, and a balanced front elevation help create the curb appeal.

Solar panels, an EV charger, custom landscaping, and a distinctive front-yard shed with a living roof make the exterior feel intentionally designed rather than simply refreshed.

Kitchen craftsmanship

Custom kitchen details built for everyday use

The kitchen includes a concealed breakfast bar cabinet, quartz work surfaces, under-cabinet outlets, LED lighting, custom Barcelona tile, and quartz window sills. These details keep the room clean visually while making the kitchen more useful day to day.

The backsplash was treated as a design feature, with outlets hidden below the cabinets so the tile can read as one continuous finish.

Main level planning

Open kitchen, dining, and living area

The main level was designed around one connected kitchen, dining, and living space. The kitchen acts as the center of the home, while the dining and living areas remain visually connected to patio access, natural light, and the yard.

This layout makes the home feel larger, brighter, and better suited for entertaining, family life, and everyday movement through the main floor.

Interior warmth

Walnut floors and a custom family room bar

Real walnut floors add warmth and character through the interior. At the back of the home, the family room includes a custom bar designed as a built-in feature rather than a standard cabinet installation.

The bar uses butcher block, open shelving, and metal supports that coordinate with the faucet finish, giving the room a crafted and personal detail.

Lower-level living

Basement ADU with flexible living space

The lower level includes a basement ADU with its own living area, kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, and laundry function. It adds a separate living environment within the property rather than feeling like leftover basement space.

One distinctive feature is the stained original concrete floor with a clear finish, preserving part of the home’s older structure while giving the ADU a unique finish.

Private suite

Primary suite, bathroom, and built-in storage

The primary suite was designed to feel grand and complete, with high ceilings, a walnut beam, layered lighting, and a connected bathroom and walk-in closet. The bathroom includes a long shower layout, bench, double vanity, and two shower heads.

Deep recessed medicine cabinets, interior lighting, outlets, shoe storage, and tailored closet zones show how the private spaces were planned with the same care as the public rooms.

Bathroom work

Bathrooms and private room finishes

Bathroom and bedroom spaces were treated as part of the full-home scope. Custom tile, updated fixtures, storage planning, and clean finish work help the private rooms feel consistent with the rest of the renovation.

The bathrooms show the level of detail that can go into smaller rooms, where tile, glass, fixtures, cabinetry, and transitions all need to be carefully coordinated.

Outdoor living

Outdoor living, stonework, and landscaping

The backyard was treated as another major part of the renovation. The outdoor space includes a stamped concrete patio designed to resemble cobblestone, basalt stone retaining walls, and an integrated stone bench built into the wall.

A bamboo garden with integrated lighting adds privacy, texture, and atmosphere, helping the yard feel like an extension of the renovated home.

What was renovated

Whole-property scope

Exterior rebuilding and refinishing, open-concept planning, a new custom kitchen, family room improvements, private rooms, bathrooms, and storage.

Custom interior details

Breakfast bar cabinet, hidden outlets, LED lighting, Barcelona tile, quartz window sills, walnut floors, custom bar, and recessed medicine cabinets.

Added living function

Basement ADU with living area, kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, laundry function, and stained original concrete flooring.

Exterior living areas

Stamped concrete patio, basalt stone walls, integrated bench, bamboo garden, landscape lighting, solar panels, EV charger, and living-roof shed.

ADU and DADU

DADU Project in View Ridge with Butterfly Roof

This View Ridge project combines three useful elements in one compact backyard build: a DADU, a custom deck, and a shed built beneath the deck. The design shows how careful planning can create a complete living environment without wasting a small footprint. The DADU also has a unique butterfly roof design, with a valley in the middle of the roof.

The DADU includes a full-size refrigerator and dishwasher, an apartment-size range, a large kitchen window, open shelving, cabinets made of rift oak, custom under-stair cabinetry, and two made-to-fit Murphy beds: one vertical and one horizontal. Those flexible sleeping solutions allow rooms to serve as offices, exercise areas, or guest rooms when the beds are closed.

Project Details

Explore how site planning, compact-space design, custom carpentry, and flexible rooms came together in this backyard DADU.

Site planning and outdoor function

Detached DADU with deck and shed integration

This project was not only a detached backyard living unit. It also included a custom deck with a shed built underneath it, giving the site layered functionality and better use of the available footprint. This layered scope added another level of construction coordination beyond a standard DADU build.

The elevated deck, the enclosed storage below it, and the surrounding stairs work together as one outdoor area.

Kitchen design

Kitchen planning in a small footprint

The kitchen is one of the best features of the DADU. Even in a compact room, the layout fits a standard-size refrigerator, dishwasher, and an apartment-size range. A large window, generous natural light, and open shelving on both sides keep the compact kitchen bright and practical.

Every inch of this small footprint was put to use, so the compact kitchen packs a lot of punch.

Custom carpentry

Custom carpentry and built-in storage

This project includes several carpentry details worth noting. Built-in cabinetry under the staircase includes a custom triangular storage door and matching trim detail. These are small but highly visible touches that make the interior feel custom rather than generic.

Every available area was used for function, which is especially important in a compact detached dwelling.

Flexible living solutions

Vertical Murphy bed for flexible living

One of the standout custom features is the first Murphy bed, built in a vertical configuration. It folds into the wall so the room can shift between sleeping space and open floor area. This type of space-saving furniture is especially valuable in a smaller DADU where every room may need to serve more than one purpose.

Silver Peak built the Murphy beds for this project, creating a tailored solution instead of relying on standard cabinetry or prefabricated storage.

Office and guest space

Horizontal Murphy bed and office / guest room flexibility

The second Murphy bed is even more distinctive. It was custom made as a horizontal Murphy bed to fit the client’s room layout and specifications. Its unusual orientation makes it a memorable example of custom problem solving for a tight room layout.

Most of the year, that room works as a clean, functional office. When guests visit, the bed folds down and the space becomes a guest bedroom. It shows how the project delivers flexible living without wasting square footage.

Exterior and bathroom details

Exterior character and compact bathroom design

The overall exterior composition, new concrete stairs, and tall entry sequence give the DADU a unique architectural presence.

Inside, Silver Peak designed a three-quarter bath upstairs and a powder room downstairs, so even the smallest spaces feel complete and usable.

Bathroom and Laundry Renovation

Accessible ADA compliant Bathroom and Laundry Renovation in Greenwood

This accessible ADA compliant bathroom and laundry renovation in Greenwood includes a curbless shower and a custom wheelchair-accessible vanity. The project needed to support multiple daily-use functions at once, including bathing, storage, circulation, privacy, and laundry access.

The strongest part of the project is how much function was built into a limited footprint. Partial-height walls, the curbless shower, the custom wheelchair-accessible vanity, warm wood storage, laundry integration, and built-in shelving all work together so the room feels organized and cohesive instead of crowded.

Project Details

Explore how the shower, vanity, storage, laundry function, and finish details were coordinated within this compact footprint.

Bathroom and laundry planning

Compact layout planned for everyday use

This Greenwood project was clarified as a bathroom and laundry renovation because the space needed to solve more than a single bathroom update. The room had to support bathing, storage, circulation, privacy, and laundry-related utility in one compact footprint.

The laundry functions as a hallway into the bathroom, and two pocket doors divide the spaces for privacy. The layout uses partial-height divider walls to organize the room without closing it off completely. That separation helps the shower, vanity, toilet, and laundry zones each feel intentional while still keeping the overall space open and easy to move through.

Vanity and finish character

Custom wheelchair-accessible vanity

The vanity was designed so that someone in a wheelchair can roll their knees under it while washing their hands. The vanity area gives the room a warmer and more custom feel. The wood counter, integrated sink, simple wall lights, and red trim detail help the space feel designed rather than treated as a basic utility room.

This is an important part of the project because compact bathrooms can easily feel purely functional. Here, the vanity adds visual character while still keeping the sink area practical for daily use.

Shower and accessibility

Curbless accessible shower with durable tile work

The curbless shower provides step-free entry and was treated as a long-term performance space. Large-format wall tile, a glass partition, a handheld fixture, grab bars, and a bench make the shower more comfortable, durable, and easier to use.

The finished shower keeps the design simple and clean while adding practical features that support safety and day-to-day function. These details help the room feel more complete than a standard cosmetic remodel.

Storage and organization

Toilet zone with upper storage and clean detailing

The toilet area was planned as its own finished zone rather than leftover space. The upper wood cabinet provides useful storage, while the surrounding wall surfaces, fixture placement, and accessories keep the area clean and organized.

This kind of detail matters in a smaller bathroom and laundry room because every wall and corner has to work harder. Storage, privacy, and fixture placement all contribute to making the room feel considered from end to end.

Laundry integration

A laundry hallway with two pocket doors

The laundry component is one of the reasons this project should be described as more than a bathroom remodel. Appliance placement, clearances, floor durability, access, and storage all had to be considered within the hallway leading into the bathroom.

The completed space shows the laundry function as part of the overall plan rather than an afterthought. It supports real household use while still maintaining the finish quality expected in a renovated bathroom.

Built-ins and final details

Shelving, lighting, and finish details complete the room

Built-in shelving, wood doors, tile flooring, lighting, and storage details help the room feel complete. The shelves add useful organization, while the warm wood tones and red edge accents connect the different parts of the room visually.

The final result is a compact bathroom and laundry space that balances utility with design. The project shows how a small service area can become a polished, organized, and durable part of the home.

Exterior Restoration and Bathroom/Laundry Renovation

Tudor Exterior Restoration and Bathroom/Laundry Renovation in Licton Springs

This Licton Springs project restored a distinctive Tudor home while also improving its bathroom and laundry spaces. Because this old-world architecture is unusual in Seattle, the curved rooflines, brickwork, stucco, and trim were especially important to preserve.

The exterior work included cleaning and re-tuckpointing the brick, replacing deteriorated mortar, removing rotten wood, rebuilding straight and curved trim, repairing stucco in the gables and rounded entry, and renewing the laundry-room entrance with a new door.

Alongside the exterior restoration, the project scope also included bathroom and laundry improvements to support better everyday use. Together, the work paired practical interior updates with a careful exterior restoration that made the whole property feel more complete.

CategoryExterior Restoration and Bathroom/Laundry Renovation
LocationLicton Springs, Seattle, Washington

Project Details

Explore how masonry repair, stucco, trim, entries, and interior utility spaces were coordinated to restore the home as one complete property.

Tudor house character

A rare Tudor home with a unique architectural identity

This Tudor home reflects a Northern European architectural style that feels uncommon in Seattle. The steep gables, brick walls, and distinctive trim made the home stand out right away, which is why the remodel needed to preserve the character instead of flattening it into a generic exterior update.

Brick restoration

Brick cleaning and re-tuck-pointing throughout the exterior

A major part of the project was restoring the brick envelope. The mortar was badly aged and, in some areas, the bricks were close to failing, so the masonry had to be cleaned and the joints carefully rebuilt.

Re-tuck-pointing the brick gave the walls structural stability again while also sharpening the look of the exterior. The same restoration continued around the chimney and side walls, not just the front facade.

Stucco and trim restoration

Gables, stucco, and detailed trim rebuilt with care

The gables and upper wall sections also needed significant attention. Rotten wood was removed, trim pieces were rebuilt, and the stucco was redone so the Tudor detailing would feel crisp again instead of tired or patched together.

The rounded and angled wood details required especially careful custom cuts.

Laundry access update

Entry improvements and a rebuilt laundry-room door

The remodel also included work at the laundry-room entry. This door area was rebuilt and fitted with a new door, improving both function and the finished appearance of the service-side access point.

Kitchen + Bathroom Remodel

Kitchen and Bathroom Remodel in Licton Springs

This Licton Springs project combines a compact kitchen and bathroom renovation where both rooms needed to do more with limited square footage. The work focused on layout, custom built-ins, storage, and finish details that make the spaces feel more open and useful.

Kitchen design

A compact kitchen designed to feel open, warm, and practical

This compact kitchen delivers impactful daily functionality. The remodel brings together blue lower cabinets, lighter wood upper cabinets, open shelving, a bright backsplash, and clean counters so the room feels layered without becoming visually overwhelming.

Built-in storage

That kind of decision is a good example of practical remodeling: small layout adjustments can dramatically improve storage, organization, and everyday usability in a compact bathroom.

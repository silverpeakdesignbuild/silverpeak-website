Whole House Renovation

Planning Beyond One Room

A whole house renovation in North Seattle is more than updating a kitchen or bathroom. It is an opportunity to look at how the entire home works and decide what needs to change for the next chapter of your life. Silver Peak approaches larger renovations by looking at the complete property, including layout, structure, building systems, exterior conditions, natural light, storage, and opportunities for an addition or ADU.

Look at the Whole Home First

One of the challenges with remodeling room by room is that decisions often affect other parts of the house. Opening a kitchen may require structural changes. A new addition can completely change the flow of the main floor. Finishing a basement could create an opportunity for an ADU. Planning these improvements together helps create a home that feels intentional rather than pieced together over time.

Older Seattle Homes Can Reveal Surprises

North Seattle is full of older homes with character, especially in neighborhoods like Greenwood, Ballard, and Phinney Ridge. That character is often worth preserving, but older homes can also come with aging electrical systems, plumbing, framing issues, foundation concerns, previous additions, or hidden damage. A whole-house plan gives the team room to evaluate those conditions in context instead of treating each room as a separate project.

Remodel, Add On, or Rebuild?

Not every home needs the same solution. Some homes already have enough space but need a better layout and updated systems. Others need additional square footage. In some older homes, existing structural conditions may make a larger rebuild the better long-term choice. A good renovation plan starts by understanding the property before deciding how much work it needs.

Why Design-Build Helps With Larger Renovations

Whole-house projects involve connected decisions. Architecture affects structure. Structure affects cost. Plumbing and electrical systems influence layouts. Windows affect both the interior and exterior. A design-build approach keeps those conversations together so the project can be evaluated from both a design and construction perspective earlier in the process.
